# CNG / Jamesons Ticketing System — Presenter Guide

Use a local demonstration environment. Do not use real employee details, private attachments, or production accounts. Keep the demonstration to one realistic example ticket and one leave request.

## Before You Present

- Sign in as a Super Admin with safe demonstration data.
- Prepare one urgent ticket with two assignees, two departments, a comment, and a private attachment.
- Prepare one pending leave request with a sample PDF or image attachment.
- Have the Dashboard, Tickets, My Work, Team Calendar, Notifications, and Leave Requests pages open in tabs.
- If email delivery is not configured, explain that alerts appear in the system and that email is enabled when the organisation's email settings are in place.
- Do not demonstrate a password reset with a real person's email address.

## Slide 1 — CNG / Jamesons Issues Ticketing System

### Say

“This is one shared place for our teams to record workplace issues, assign responsibility, follow progress, and see what needs attention. The local build is complete. The remaining step is approval to roll it out to the live system.”

### Show

- The login page or Dashboard landing page.

### Test to demonstrate

- Sign in successfully with a demonstration account.

## Slide 2 — What Problem It Solves

### Say

“The goal is simple: no issue should be lost in email or chat. Every request has an owner, a priority, and a clear record of what happened. Teams can work together while still seeing only the information appropriate to their role.”

### Show

- Open the prepared ticket and point out its subject, priority, assignees, involved departments, and activity history.

### Test to demonstrate

- Create a harmless sample ticket with a clear subject and one department.
- Add a second assignee and confirm that both names appear on the ticket.

## Slide 3 — Core Ticket Workflow

### Say

“The working cycle is create, assign, work, collaborate, track, and resolve. The important outcome is that we can always see who owns the next action and whether a ticket is waiting too long.”

### Show

- Change the prepared ticket from Open to In Progress.
- Add a normal comment and show the activity history.
- Point out the ticket age and attention indicator.

### Test to demonstrate

- Change the sample ticket to Pending and use Follow up.
- Confirm the activity record is updated and the in-system alert appears.

## Slide 4 — Main User Roles

### Say

“Access follows job responsibility. Super Admins manage the system. Team Leaders work only on tickets assigned to them. CNG Admins can review and filter tickets but cannot change them. Team Members use Leave Requests and Notifications, not the main ticketing area.”

### Show

- Briefly show the Super Admin navigation.
- If demonstration accounts are available, switch to the Team Leader and CNG Admin accounts to show their different menus.

### Test to demonstrate

- With a Team Leader account, open an assigned ticket successfully.
- Confirm a ticket assigned to someone else does not appear.
- With a CNG Admin account, confirm that viewing and filtering work but Create, Edit, and Export are unavailable.

## Slide 5 — Ticket Features

### Say

“Tickets can involve more than one person and department, without losing clear ownership. The system also makes urgent, overdue, idle, and unassigned work easy to find. Search, filters, exports, comments, and alerts support everyday follow-up.”

### Show

- Show the Tickets list filtered to urgent or overdue work.
- Open the prepared ticket and show multiple assignees and departments.
- Show one comment and the alert area.

### Test to demonstrate

- Filter for urgent tickets, then clear the filter.
- Show that responsibility can be shared across the prepared ticket's assignees.

## Slide 6 — Dashboard & Reporting

### Say

“The Dashboard gives managers a quick picture of current work: volume, status, urgency, tickets needing attention, and recent activity. The cards open the matching ticket list, so a summary can immediately lead to action.”

### Show

- Dashboard counts and recent activity.
- Click the Urgent or Overdue card and show the matching filtered ticket list.
- Show the trend chart and reports page.

### Test to demonstrate

- Change the Dashboard date range and confirm the counts update.
- Click one card and confirm the resulting list matches that category.

## Slide 7 — Leave, Calendar & Attendance

### Say

“The system also supports workforce coordination. A Team Member submits a leave request with supporting information. A Team Leader reviews it first, then a Department Head gives final approval. Approved leave, holidays, events, and attendance information appear together on the calendar.”

### Show

- The prepared leave request and its approval status.
- Team Calendar with a holiday, an event, and approved leave.
- A calendar item's details panel.

### Test to demonstrate

- Submit a sample leave request with a non-sensitive sample attachment.
- Approve it as Team Leader, then as Department Head.
- Confirm it appears on the Team Calendar after final approval.

## Slide 8 — Security & Privacy Controls

### Say

“The system protects accounts, checks access before sensitive actions, and keeps confidential files private. It is designed so that access is enforced by the system itself, not just hidden from the screen.”

### Show

- A confidential attachment on the prepared ticket.
- The private-comment option, if appropriate for the audience.

### Test to demonstrate

- With an account that lacks attachment access, try to open the sample attachment and show that access is denied.
- Attempt to open an administration page with a non-admin account and show that access is denied.

## Slide 9 — Administration & Operating Routine

### Say

“Super Admins have dedicated tools to manage people, access, restored tickets, audit history, calendar items, imports, reports, and system checks. The suggested routine turns the system into a regular management habit: daily follow-up, weekly workload review, and monthly health checks.”

### Show

- Super Admin navigation.
- Audit log or Users module with demonstration data only.
- The system-health page, if available.

### Test to demonstrate

- Add or update a demonstration user only if it can be safely removed afterward.
- Prefer the safer alternative: show an existing demonstration user and its access settings without changing them.

## Slide 10 — Business Value and Status

### Say

“The business value is fewer missed follow-ups, clearer ownership, and better visibility across teams. The core service has been live since 25 July. The expanded local build was completed on 5 August. Production rollout is the open item and needs approval, deployment access, and final live checks.”

### Show

- Return to the Dashboard and the prepared ticket as the closing example.

### Close with

“The requested decision is approval for the production rollout. After rollout, we will complete the live checks for roles, dashboard figures, attachments, leave approvals, and the calendar.”

## Short Demonstration Run-Through

If time is limited, use this five-minute flow:

1. Sign in and open the Dashboard.
2. Open an urgent sample ticket; show owners, departments, status, and activity.
3. Click an urgent or overdue Dashboard card and show the matching list.
4. Show a leave request moving through Team Leader and Department Head approval.
5. Open the calendar and show the approved leave entry.
6. Close with the rollout decision and the final live checks.

## Live Rollout Checks After Approval

- Super Admin can access Dashboard, Tickets, Notifications, and administration.
- Team Leader sees only assigned tickets and can use My Work and leave approvals.
- CNG Admin can view and filter tickets but cannot change, export, or administer them.
- Team Member can submit and track leave requests and use Notifications, with no main ticketing access.
- Dashboard cards, attachments, leave approvals, calendar items, notifications, and follow-up actions work as expected.

## Important Presenter Notes

- Say “local build complete; rollout pending approval,” not “fully live,” for the newer features.
- Avoid giving a precise technical deployment count: the project records disagree on the final change set. The decision needed is still clear—approve the rollout and complete the live checks.
- If asked about deferred items, say that advanced account protection and external integrations are future options, not part of this rollout.
